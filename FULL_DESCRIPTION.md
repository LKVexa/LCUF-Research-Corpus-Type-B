# Full Description of LCUF Type A and Type B

Copyright (C) 2026 RUSSELL PHILIP SMITHSON

LCUF, the Columned Unikernel and Hyperfederated Fabric Language research corpus, defines a program representation in which computation, ownership, authority, ordering, resources and evidence appear explicitly in source. Its purpose is to provide a technically inspectable foundation for developing compact execution environments and federated computational systems. The corpus connects formal language rules, the author's JA mathematical foundation, executable host references, traceable architecture requirements and reproducible evidence.

The two editions have distinct roles. **Type A is version 0.1.0**, the foundational formal and mathematical corpus. **Type B is version 0.2.0**, the article-applied extension that preserves Type A and introduces additional computational profiles and architecture obligations. The designation expresses an edition relationship, not a replacement of the original arithmetic or a claim that either edition has completed native or physical implementation.

## Columned program representation

LCUF organizes source into twelve columns: row identity, site, profile, operation, inputs, output, type, effects, capabilities, ordering constraints, budget and evidence. The arrangement makes the conditions under which a computation is admitted visible alongside the computation itself. A program declares a finite dependency graph; the host reference checks that graph and its policy before evaluating admitted rows.

Ownership identifies the declared site associated with a value or operation. Capabilities and effects describe permitted activity. Ordering constraints and data dependencies determine execution readiness. The references enforce defined inputs, single output writers, compatible operation types, permitted copies, acyclic dependencies and finite model budgets. Deterministic lexical selection among ready rows yields reproducible local execution order. Canonical intermediate representations and receipt chains connect the normalized contract to observed outputs.

These mechanisms supply an executable research model of explicit admission and evidence. Native implementations must separately establish that their allocators, drivers, ABI boundaries, clocks, transport and external effects satisfy the corresponding contracts.

## Type A foundational corpus

Type A establishes the finite `LCUF/0.1` language and its Python host reference. Its closed operation set contains `const`, `add`, `mul`, `copy`, `assert_eq` and `emit`. It supports `i64`, `bool` and `text` values, with `unit` for terminal results. Signed integer limits, exact declared authority, deterministic dataflow, normalized identities, modeled resources and local receipt chains provide the implemented core.

Eight formal volumes describe grammar, static types and authority, operational semantics, resources, identity, scoped propositions, diagnostics and extension contracts. Nine systems and institutional research volumes address the architecture, native execution, federation, threat model, evaluation, data governance, assurance, scenario design and primary references. A mathematical volume preserves the author's JA foundation. Together these form eighteen primary technical volumes and 41,309 words of curated Markdown, measured as whitespace-separated tokens including tables and notation.

The foundational evidence includes a 10,144-record core conformance master, 1,619 mathematical operation and input cases, 500 numerical comparisons and 92 original tests. Analytical expectations, deliberate rejection mutations, metamorphic relationships and source-bound receipts distinguish different forms of evidence. Parameterized case populations remain related constructions; their size alone does not establish independent experiments or exhaustive correctness.

Type A also develops native and federation requirements. The native contracts cover dependency closure, target identity, ABI qualification, drivers, build artifacts, boot evidence and resources. Federation contracts cover membership, delegated authority, routing, durable effects, idempotency, revocation and recovery. Their scenario catalogs state the setup, stimulus, observer and oracle required for future experiments.

## Preserved JA mathematical foundation

The mathematical foundation is the **8S Penteract–S³ Master Coupled Mechanics Law**. The corpus preserves the supplied figure and frozen reference files, the five base coordinates indexed 4 through 8, and the coupled acceptance gate `eta_ind >= epsilon_ind AND Delta8S > epsilon_gain`.

Its treatment separates finite real matrix geometry, local S³ and projector relationships, author-defined coupling and engineering admission checks. Interpretations of `Classify` and `Score` stay unresolved where the supplied mathematical authority does not define them. Research tolerances and finite-value checks carry their own versioned policy rather than becoming retroactive changes to the author's law. The future typed adapter specifies the conditions under which these quantities could enter a fabric program.

## Type B article-applied extension

Type B preserves the complete Type A corpus byte-exact and adds `LCUF-X/0.2`, a separately identified host research language. It incorporates an end-to-end assessment of twenty supplied research files, including the supplied 1-bit LAMB paper. Three findings registries retain 147 claim dispositions and 82 architecture requirements. Twenty-four extension requirements connect those analyses to concrete language and admission contracts.

Five new design volumes develop the extension grammar and admission rules, p-adic geometry, signal and image profiles, optimizer and unikernel contracts, and integrated architecture. Type B therefore contains twenty-three primary technical volumes and 60,775 words of curated documentation. The original source materials and numbered extracts remain available for tracing individual decisions.

Its twenty-one total operations extend the six core operations with explicit finite quotient arithmetic, conversions, reduction, capped valuation, coset averaging, HRR operations, cyclic permutation, bounded diffusion, raster indexing and compression components. New constructors distinguish finite residues, real vectors, sampled fields, decoded image arrays, transport packets and retained optimizer state.

Finite residue calculations occur in `Z/(p^n)` with explicit prime and precision parameters. They are distinct from the full p-adic field and from finite extension fields. `PField` places real-valued samples on finite residue addresses, allowing bounded coset averaging without implying continuous p-adic diffusion. Real-valued HRR convolution and correlation support distributed representations while retaining approximate unbinding and role-order limitations. A periodic convex diffusion stencil supplies a finite host operator whose stability domain is explicit.

Raster operations act on decoded unsigned 8-bit samples with declared dimensions and exact indexing. Container decoding, scientific sample metadata, time units, palette disposal, calibration and codec provenance belong to separate adapters. Image-format GIF and Gaussian Interaction Frame terminology occupy separate namespaces. Optical propagation, intensity readback and phase reconstruction require their own operator, unit and measurement contracts.

The scaled-sign compression component separates a transportable wire packet from the local error-feedback residual. Explicit ownership and capability checks govern packet movement. Compression phases, frozen-state identity, step counters and topology epochs inform the optimizer architecture. Complete distributed 1-bit LAMB additionally requires the full update equations, worker and aggregate feedback, variance states, layer scaling, collectives and durable checkpoints; the host component does not supply complete training.

## Evidence and implementation status

Type B adds 352 conformance records across 55 construction families, comprising 272 accepted programs, 32 representative rejection mechanisms and 48 metamorphic pairs. Those records account for 400 source evaluation slots. It also adds 208 numerical cross-checks, 60 unit tests and detection of five specified in-memory faults. Numerical tolerances, domains, seeds and runtime versions remain attached to their results.

The release records independent implementation and validator review, deterministic reconstruction of the extension artifacts, preservation of the embedded baseline and successful host validation from a fresh archive extraction. Baseline evidence remains identifiable as inherited Type A evidence rather than being relabeled as newly executed Type B experiments. Integrity manifests bind file populations and hashes to a trusted copy; they are unsigned.

Native boot images, authenticated federation, full optimizer training, image-container decoders and calibrated physical implementations remain specified development work. The architecture names the dependencies and evidence required to advance them. This includes native resource enforcement and buffer lifetimes; authenticated identities and capability attenuation; durable collective and effect state; reproducible decoder metadata; and measurement-specific calibration, uncertainty and observer information.

## Research and publication use

Type A provides the stable formal core for inspecting the language and JA foundation. Type B provides the expanded host profiles and source-to-architecture analysis for developing additional computational domains. Researchers can follow a claim from a supplied source to a findings registry, a requirement, an implemented operation or a proposed experiment, and then inspect the associated evidence and limits.

The corpus supplies structured governance, reproducibility and assurance material suitable for technical review. Institutional language describes these structures; it does not claim an external affiliation, accreditation, certification or completed peer review. Contributions should preserve this connection between the claim made, the implementation supplied and the observation recorded.
